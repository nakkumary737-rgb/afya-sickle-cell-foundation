Brian’s Journey: From Frequent Admissions to Top of Class

📍 Wakiso District, Uganda

For years, 12-year-old Brian missed almost half of every school term due to sudden, severe painful crises. His mother, a single parent, spent nights in tears at local clinics, overwhelmed by medical costs and fear for her son's future.

When Brian was introduced to the AFYA Sickle Cell Foundation, everything shifted. Through regular clinical reviews with Afya Medicare, a structured hydration plan, and routine preventative care, Brian’s crisis episodes reduced dramatically. Today, he hasn't missed a day of school in over six months!

"Before AFYA, I thought I would never finish primary school. Now I want to study hard and become a doctor to help other warriors like me." — Brian

Grace's Voice: Overcoming Stigma and Isolation

📍 Mukono District, Uganda

When Grace discovered both of her twin daughters had sickle cell disease, neighbors blamed her, and isolation followed. Overwhelmed and heartbroken, she struggled to manage their health alone without reliable medical advice.

Joining the AFYA Parent Support Circle gave Grace a community that understood her daily struggles. With guidance from founders Kittengo Alysious & Nanyonjo Noeline, plus direct clinical support from Afya Medicare, Grace learned home crisis management and gained the courage to stand tall.

"AFYA didn't just give my daughters medical care—they gave me my dignity back as a mother. I am no longer ashamed; I am proud of my warriors!" — Grace

Emmanuel: Championing Sickle Cell Awareness in Schools

📍 Kampala, Uganda

Growing up, Emmanuel hid his diagnosis from classmates out of fear of being mocked or excluded from sports. The emotional toll was as heavy as the physical pain.

Through AFYA’s youth advocacy program, Emmanuel embraced his story. Today, he walks into secondary schools across Kampala alongside the AFYA team, educating young people on genotype testing and showing younger students that warriors can achieve anything.

"Sickle cell is part of my journey, but it isn't my destination. AFYA taught me to wear my warrior badge with pride." — Emmanuel
